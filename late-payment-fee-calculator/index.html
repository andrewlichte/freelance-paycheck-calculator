How late fees on invoices work

A late fee is only enforceable if the client agreed to it before the work started, so put it in your contract or proposal and repeat it on every invoice, for example: "Payments received after the due date incur a late fee of 1.5% per month." If it was never agreed, you can still ask, but treat it as a bargaining chip rather than a debt.

Common ways to charge

  • 1 to 1.5% per month on the unpaid balance. This is the most common choice and easy to explain.
  • A flat fee such as $25 or 5% of the invoice, charged once the invoice is late.
  • Statutory interest. Some places set a rate by law. In the UK, for example, businesses can claim 8% plus the Bank of England base rate under the late payment legislation.

Many US states cap interest rates, and the limits vary, so check your state's rules before you charge more than about 1.5% a month.

Common questions

What is a reasonable late fee for a freelance invoice?

1 to 1.5% of the unpaid amount per month is the most common, or a one-off flat fee of around $25 to $50 for smaller invoices. Agree it in your contract first and keep it within your state's or country's legal limits.

Can I add a late fee if it wasn't in my contract?

You can ask, but the client usually doesn't have to pay a fee they never agreed to. Add a late fee clause to future contracts and invoices so it applies next time.

How do I calculate 1.5% per month interest on an invoice?

Multiply the invoice amount by 0.015, then by the number of days late divided by 30. A $2,400 invoice that is 45 days late comes to $2,400 × 0.015 × 1.5 = $54.

When should I send a late payment reminder?

Send a friendly reminder a few days before the due date, another the day after it passes, then follow up weekly. Stay polite and specific: the invoice number, amount, due date and how to pay.
