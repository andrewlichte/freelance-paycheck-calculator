How this calculator works

  1. Self-employment tax. 15.3% on 92.35% of your profit: 12.4% Social Security (up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, less any W-2 wages) and 2.9% Medicare.
  2. Deductions. Half of your self-employment tax, the 2026 standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly), and the 20% qualified business income deduction when your taxable income is under the threshold.
  3. Income tax. The 2026 federal brackets from 10% to 37% applied to what is left.
  4. Split it in four. Subtract tax already withheld from wages and divide the rest across the four due dates.

It leaves out credits (such as the child tax credit), itemized deductions, the 0.9% additional Medicare tax on high earners and other income like investments, so treat the result as a planning estimate.

Common questions

Who has to pay quarterly estimated taxes?

In the US, you generally need to pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year after withholding and credits. Most freelancers, contractors and sole proprietors with steady profit fall into this group.

How much should I set aside for taxes as a freelancer?

Many self-employed people set aside 25 to 30% of each payment. The calculator above shows your own figure per $1,000 earned, based on 2026 brackets and self-employment tax.

What happens if I miss a quarterly payment?

The IRS charges an underpayment penalty, which works like interest on the amount you should have paid, from the due date until you pay. Paying late is still better than not paying at all, because the penalty keeps growing.

What is the safe harbor rule?

You generally avoid the underpayment penalty if your payments during the year cover at least 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your adjusted gross income was over $150,000), or 90% of this year's tax. Enter last year's tax in the calculator to see that amount.

What is self-employment tax?

It is Social Security and Medicare tax for people who work for themselves. Employees split it with their employer, but freelancers pay both halves: 15.3% on 92.35% of profit, with the Social Security part stopping at the yearly wage base ($184,500 for 2026).
