How to work out your freelance hourly rate

Most new freelancers take their old salary, divide by 2,080 hours and charge that. It almost always leaves them underpaid, because as a freelancer you pay your own tax and costs, you take unpaid time off, and a good chunk of every week goes on admin, sales and email that no client pays for.

  1. Start with the money you want to keep. That is your take-home pay for the year.
  2. Add tax and business costs. Gross up the take-home for tax, then add software, equipment, insurance and anything your employer used to pay.
  3. Count the hours you can actually bill. Weeks worked, times hours per week, times the share of hours that are billable. 50 to 70% is typical.
  4. Divide. Revenue needed divided by billable hours is the lowest rate that gets you there.

The formula

Hourly rate = (take-home ÷ (1 − tax rate) + business costs) ÷ (weeks worked × hours per week × billable %)

With the example numbers above: $60,000 take-home at 28% tax needs $83,333 of profit, plus $4,000 of costs is $87,333. 46 weeks × 40 hours × 65% is 1,196 billable hours, so the minimum rate is about $73 an hour.

Common questions

What is a good hourly rate for a freelancer?

There is no single number, but it should cover your take-home pay, your tax, your business costs and your unbillable time. Use the calculator to find your floor, then check what others in your field charge and price at or above it.

How many billable hours does a freelancer have per year?

Usually between 1,000 and 1,400. A full-time employee is paid for about 2,080 hours, but freelancers lose time to sales, admin, invoicing and unpaid time off, so only 50 to 70% of working hours end up billable.

How do I convert a salary to a freelance hourly rate?

A quick rule of thumb is to divide the salary by 1,000 to 1,200 instead of 2,080. A $70,000 salary becomes roughly $60 to $70 an hour. The calculator above gives a more exact figure from your own tax rate, costs and time off.

Should I charge hourly or a day rate?

Hourly works well for small or unclear jobs. Day rates and project prices reward you for working quickly and are easier for clients to budget. Many freelancers work out an hourly rate first, then quote day rates or fixed prices built from it.
